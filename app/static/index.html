How estimates are computed

Inviscid solution. A multi-element Hess–Smith panel method solves the exact inviscid flow around all elements at once, in free air and in ground effect (method of images). These numbers are exact for inviscid flow and validated against independent formulations, but inviscid ground effect grows without bound as ride height shrinks — real flows saturate.

Corrected estimate. C_est = η_visc · [C_free + k_g·(C_ground − C_free)], where η_visc (default 0.85) is the free-air viscous realization and k_g = 0.85·tanh(1.4/0.85·h/c) realizes only part of the inviscid ground-effect gain — vanishing as the wing approaches the road, where the inviscid model diverges but real flow chokes. Both knobs are under Air properties & calibration — recalibrate them against RANS or tunnel data when available.

Element loading. Each element's free-air inviscid load (×η) is checked against its isolated CLmax from NeuralFoil at the element's own Reynolds number — the classical high-lift load budget. Warnings start at 90%, criticals at 110%.

Drag. Two parts. Profile drag: each element's section drag from its polar, looked up at the ground-effect operating point. Induced drag — usually the larger part: computed from the same downforce the tool reports, CDi = CL²/(π·AR·e)·φ, where e is the span efficiency (Air properties & calibration) and φ = (16h/b)²/(1+(16h/b)²) is the classical ground-effect reduction — wings near the road shed much weaker tip vortices. Interference and support drag are not modeled.
