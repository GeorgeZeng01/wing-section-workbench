How estimates are computed

Inviscid solution. A multi-element Hess–Smith panel method solves the exact inviscid flow around all elements at once, in free air and in ground effect (method of images). These numbers are exact for inviscid flow and validated against independent formulations, but inviscid ground effect grows without bound as ride height shrinks — real flows saturate.

Corrected estimate. C_est = η_visc · [C_free + G_real], where η_visc (default 0.85) is the free-air viscous realization and G_real is the realized share of the inviscid ground-effect gain: the gain is saturated at a cap (3× the free-air load) and choked near the road by tanh((h/c)/0.045), so the estimate stays bounded toward the ground. RANS cross-referencing (2026-07, docs/calibration) validated the estimate on the baseline section at h/c 0.086 and 0.429, measured it optimistic below the choke onset (−19% at h/c 0.071; a standing warning fires below h/c 0.08) and conservative through the mid-height gain peak (up to +67% at h/c 0.171 — the truth model's load peaks nearer h/c ≈ 0.17 than the published-experiment 0.06–0.1 this curve was originally shaped to; more downforce than claimed, so deliberately not a warning). Both η_visc and the gain-realization factor k_g are editable under Air properties & calibration; leave k_g blank for the automatic ride-height curve, or pin it 0–1 against RANS or tunnel data. The advanced constants gain_cap_ratio and choke_h_c are available at the config/API level.

Element loading. Each element's free-air inviscid load (×η) is checked against its isolated CLmax from NeuralFoil at the element's own Reynolds number — the classical high-lift load budget. Warnings start at 90%, criticals at 110%.

Drag. Two parts. Profile drag: each element's section drag from its polar, looked up at the ground-effect operating point. Induced drag — usually the larger part: computed from the same downforce the tool reports, CDi = CL²/(π·AR·e)·φ, where e is the span efficiency (Air properties & calibration) and φ = (16h/b)²/(1+(16h/b)²) is the classical ground-effect reduction — wings near the road shed much weaker tip vortices. Interference and support drag are not modeled.
